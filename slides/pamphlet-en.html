Early Signs & Red Flags CDC Milestones (Selected Ages, 0–5 Years)

👀Limited Eye Contact
👆No Pointing to Show
💬Delayed Speech
🔁Repetitive Behaviors
🧸No Pretend Play
📢Sensory Sensitivities
⚠️ "Golden Rule" (Any Age): Any regression or loss of speech, babbling, eye contact, gestures, or social skills at any age: contact your pediatrician promptly. Do not wait for the next well-child visit.
🧩 Social, Play & Communication (CDC 2022)
By ~9 Months: Does not respond to name
12 Months: No babbling · Not waving bye-bye · Does not play peek-a-boo
15 Months: Not pointing to ask · Does not bring/show toys to share excitement
16–18 Months: No single words · Not pointing to show by 18 months
By ~30 Months: Little or no pretend play
Behavioral Patterns: Hand-flapping, rocking, spinning · Extreme distress at everyday sounds or lights
Note: Milestone ages follow CDC 2022 revised checklists; children develop at different rates. Checklists do not replace formal screening. If concerned, talk to your pediatrician.
🧩 Multidimensional Spectrum Wheel Autism is a broad spectrum where developmental domains vary independently. Each child forms a unique profile, highlighting strengths while guiding tailored support.
📋 4 Steps to Seek Support
1Observe & Track: Note specific concerns with milestone checklists. Checklists do not replace formal screening.
2Talk to Your Pediatrician: Ask for an M-CHAT-R/F screen (and developmental screening). You can request extra screening anytime if concerned.
3Comprehensive Assessment: Moderate/high risk prompts referral for full evaluation.
4Start Early Intervention: Contact Fairfax ITC (703) 246-7121 (ages 0–3) or FCPS Child Find 571-423-4121 (20 months–5 years). A suspected delay is enough to refer; no formal diagnosis needed, and no SSN needed to make a referral.
❓ Misconceptions & Clinical Facts
Myth
"Children who can speak cannot have autism."
Fact
Autism is a broad spectrum. Many autistic children speak fluently but need support with social reciprocity, nonverbal cues, or sensory regulation.
Myth
"The child is young; let us wait and see if they outgrow it."
Fact
Earlier support is generally associated with better outcomes. Waiting can delay help during a period of rapid brain growth. If you are concerned, contact your pediatrician or ITC promptly.
Myth
"Autism is caused by cold parenting or vaccines."
Fact
Autism is an innate neurodevelopmental difference. Decades of research refute links to parenting styles or vaccines.

Autism is an innate neurodevelopmental difference, unrelated to parenting or vaccines. For ages 0–5, earlier identification and support are generally linked with better long-term outcomes.
