Milestones & Warning Signs

Early Signs & Developmental Red Flags

Autism is a neurodevelopmental condition that can be reliably identified as early as 18–24 months. Recognizing warning signs allows parents to access intervention services during the most critical periods of early brain development.

1 Social & Communication Milestones

  • • No response to name: Does not turn or respond when their name is called by 12 months (often mistaken for hearing difficulties).
  • • Lack of joint attention: Does not point to show you something interesting (e.g., pointing to an airplane or toy) by 18 months, or does not look when you point.
  • • No sharing of interests: Does not bring or show toys/objects to parents just to share excitement by 15 months.
  • • No imitation: Does not imitate simple actions, sounds, or facial expressions by 18 months.
  • • No interactive play: Does not play simple interactive games (like peek-a-boo, pat-a-cake) or wave "bye-bye" by 12 months.

2 Play & Behavioral Patterns

  • • Absence of pretend play: Does not engage in imaginative play (e.g., pretending to feed a doll, talk on a toy phone, or push a block like a car) by 18–24 months.
  • • Repetitive behaviors: Repeatedly flaps hands, rocks their body, or spins in circles.
  • • Unusual play focus: Plays with toys in unusual ways, such as repeatedly spinning the wheels of a toy car or lining up objects instead of playing with them, getting highly distressed if their order is disrupted.
  • • Sensory sensitivities: Showing extreme distress at everyday sounds (like vacuum cleaners or blenders), or having unusual fascinations with lights or spinning objects.

The "Golden Rule" Red Flag (Any Age)

Any regression or loss of skills (including speech, babbling, eye contact, gestures, or social abilities at any age) is a significant red flag that requires immediate developmental evaluation by a pediatrician.
