Early signs to watch for

  • No big smiles or joyful expressions by 6 months
  • No back-and-forth sharing of sounds, smiles, or facial expressions by 9 months
  • No babbling by 12 months; loss of language or social skills at any age
  • Poor eye contact, limited pointing or gestures

Government & Medical Authority Resources

Authoritative guidance from U.S. government and health organizations:

  • CDC – Screening for Autism Spectrum Disorder — Covers developmental monitoring, screening recommendations at 18 and 24 months, and the diagnostic process.
  • CDC – Clinical Screening for ASD (for healthcare providers) — Details on screening tools, AAP recommendations, and clinic workflow.
  • CDC – Accessing Services for ASD — Guide to finding and accessing early intervention services.
  • CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early" — Free milestone checklists and a Milestone Tracker app for parents.
  • NIH / NICHD: Autism and Early Intervention — Research-backed information on early intervention.
  • NIH / PMC – Screening for ASD in Early Childhood — Comprehensive research review of screening tools (M-CHAT), global recommendations, and early intervention evidence.
  • World Health Organization — Autism Spectrum Disorders — Global information and guidance.
  • U.S. Department of Education — IDEA (Early Intervention) — Individuals with Disabilities Education Act resources for infants and toddlers.
  • American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) — Guidance on developmental screening and pediatric practice.

Screening & Early Intervention Services

Free screening programs (ChildFind), pediatric developmental screenings, and local early intervention services can evaluate concerns — earlier is better. If you have concerns, ask your pediatrician or contact ChildFind.

  • FCPS Special Education Registration (Child Find) — Local screening and referral resources (check your state for similar programs).
  • M-CHAT-R/F (Modified Checklist for Autism in Toddlers) — The most widely studied Level 1 screening tool for toddlers 16–30 months.
  • ASQ (Ages and Stages Questionnaire) — Broad developmental screening tool used in pediatric practice.

The Most Important Local Number to Know

  • Fairfax-Falls Church Community Services Board — 703-324-4400 — The single most important number. The CSB is the "point of entry" for residents of Fairfax County and the cities of Fairfax and Falls Church to apply for services for developmental disabilities including ASD. Eligible families receive Support Coordination, Day Support, and Employment Services. No Social Security number is required to qualify. This number unlocks everything — ITC-VA, DD waivers, school referrals.

China-specific resources: Rural Hubei

For families in rural Hubei Province, China — particularly relevant for parents who return to visit family or for referral families:

3-Tier Screening System (乡镇 → 县级 → 专科医院)

Under China's 2022 national framework:

  • Township level — 乡镇卫生院 (township health centers) handle initial autism screening
  • County level — 县级妇幼保健机构 (county-level maternal and child health hospitals) handle re-screening
  • Specialized hospitals — Handle diagnosis and rehabilitation

Key message for your pamphlet: "你孩子的乡镇卫生院提供免费发育筛查。请主动要求医生检查孤独症预警征象。" (Your township clinic offers free developmental screenings. Proactively ask the doctor to check for autism warning signs.)

Free Checkups Already Exist — Parents Just Don't Know

  • Since 2009, China's national public health program provides 13 free health checkups for all children ages 0–6 at township clinics, including behavioral and developmental assessments.
  • Over 80 million children receive these free checkups annually.
  • Any child flagged during checkups is referred to county-level maternal/child health institutions.
  • The gap isn't the service — it's that rural parents don't know these checkups include autism behavioral flags. Your pamphlet closes that gap directly.

Provincial Referral Destination

  • 湖北省妇幼保健院 (Hubei Maternal and Child Health Hospital) in Wuhan — The provincial-level destination for autism diagnosis and rehabilitation. Families who receive a positive county-level screen should be directed here.

Free Online Resources for Families Anywhere in China

  • 星星雨 / Alsolife — China's oldest autism organization. Free online resources including a "5-Day New Parent Course" (新家长5天必修课), developmental milestone guides, and screening information accessible by phone nationwide. Particularly useful because rural families can access it on a phone even without clinic access.

2024–2028 National Action Plan

  • In 2024, China's Disabled Persons' Federation jointly issued the "Autism Children Care Promotion Action Plan 2024–2028" — the first national framework for lifelong autism support, covering early screening, rehabilitation services, and long-term support infrastructure. This is useful framing for your Hubei workshop to show families that the government recognizes the problem and services are expanding.
